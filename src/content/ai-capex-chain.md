## Definition

<div class="def-box">
<p class="definition-text">
<dfn id="capex-def"><strong>Capital expenditure (capex)</strong></dfn> is spending on long-lived physical assets such as buildings, machinery and equipment, recorded on the balance sheet and depreciated over years rather than expensed immediately. The <strong>AI capex chain</strong> is the sequence by which one company's data centre spending becomes revenue for its suppliers, and their suppliers in turn.
</p>
<div class="def-source">Source: Company capital expenditure guidance, Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta Platforms, fiscal 2026.</div>
</div>

Artificial intelligence runs in buildings. Training and serving large models requires warehouses of accelerators, the electricity to run them, the cooling to stop them overheating and the fibre to connect them. Every dollar committed to that infrastructure leaves the buyer's balance sheet and lands on a supplier's income statement.

That transfer is the entire link between "AI is important" and "these stocks went up." It is not a story about model capability. It is an accounting identity: capex out, revenue in.

## How the Capex Chain Works

<tr><td><strong>2. Power</strong></td><td>Generation, transmission, transformers, switchgear, backup, cooling</td><td>Longest — multi-year interconnection queues</td><td>Grid connection approval</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>3. Networking</strong></td><td>Optics, switches, cabling, interconnect silicon</td><td>Medium — tracks deployment schedule</td><td>Cluster size and topology</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>

**Compute.** The largest single share of a data centre's cost is the accelerator, but the chain runs deeper than one supplier. Each accelerator requires high-bandwidth memory, advanced packaging capacity, foundry wafer starts and substrate supply. The bottleneck moves around: when memory is scarce, memory producers capture the margin. Microsoft disclosed that roughly $25 billion of its approximately $190 billion 2026 capital expenditure is component price inflation — meaning a material share of hyperscaler spending is now being transferred to component suppliers through price rather than volume.

**Power.** A large AI campus draws power at the scale of a small city. Meta's Hyperion campus in Louisiana is designed to draw up to 5 GW at peak. That demand supports generators, utilities, transformer and switchgear manufacturers, and cooling suppliers — but power is where the chain is slowest and least elastic, because grid interconnection queues and transmission build-out run on multi-year regulatory timelines that no amount of capital compresses.

**Networking.** Training a large model requires thousands of accelerators to exchange data continuously, which makes interconnect a performance constraint rather than a commodity. Optical transceivers, switching silicon and cabling scale with the size and topology of clusters.

<div class="callout callout-teal">
<div class="callout-label">Why Supplier Stocks Move First</div>
<p class="callout-text">Suppliers' share prices reprice on <em>announcements</em>, because a stock price discounts expected future profits. A capex guidance raise changes the forecast for every company in the chain immediately, months or years before any of that revenue is recognised. This is why the chain can rally on a single earnings call and sell off on a single cancelled project — the price is trading the promise, not the shipment.</p>
</div>

For scale on the trajectory: Amazon's approximately $200 billion for 2026 compares with roughly $96.5 billion spent in 2025 and $83 billion in 2024. Meta raised its full-year 2026 guidance mid-year, citing higher component pricing and additional data centre costs.

The capital intensity figures are the ones that matter for risk. A company spending 54% of its revenue on physical assets has committed to a future in which that capacity earns a return. If the return does not materialise, the depreciation still does — it flows through the income statement for years regardless.

## How to Use Capex Data in Practice

**1. Read capex guidance as the leading indicator for the whole chain.** Rising guidance signals supplier revenue growth ahead; a flattening or cut signals the reverse, and it reaches supplier share prices long before it reaches supplier revenue.

**2. Separate revenue recognised from revenue promised.** A supplier's backlog, book-to-bill ratio and remaining performance obligations tell you what has been contracted. A customer's capex guidance tells you what has been announced. Announcements are revised; backlog is harder to cancel without penalty.

**3. Distinguish price inflation from volume growth.** Microsoft's disclosure that roughly $25 billion of its 2026 capex is component price inflation means a meaningful part of the spending increase buys no additional capacity. For a component supplier that is margin; for the buyer it is cost with no return attached.

**4. Check where a supplier sits on the lag curve.** Compute suppliers convert orders in quarters. Power infrastructure converts over years, constrained by interconnection queues. Two companies exposed to the same theme can have completely different revenue timing, and the market frequently prices them as though they do not.

**5. Measure your own concentration across the chain.** A chip company, a utility with data centre exposure and a networking supplier look like three separate positions. If all three depend on the same capex assumption, they are one position held three times. Cluenex covers the top 1,000+ US-listed stocks, which allows that shared dependency to be examined name by name.

**6. Watch the constraints, not the ambitions.** Announced project pipelines exceed what the grid, the permitting process and local politics will permit. The binding constraint on AI buildout in 2026 is not capital.

## Common Mistakes and Misconceptions

<div class="callout callout-red">
<div class="callout-label">✗ Mistake 1</div>
<p class="callout-text"><strong>"Announced capex is committed revenue."</strong><br>
Capex guidance is a forecast a company revises quarterly. Meta raised 2026 guidance mid-year; guidance can move down as easily as up. Projects also fail after announcement for reasons unrelated to the buyer's finances — permitting, litigation, power availability, local opposition. Between announcement and revenue sit several ways for the number to change.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout callout-red">
<div class="callout-label">✗ Mistake 2</div>
<p class="callout-text"><strong>"A cancelled project proves the AI boom is ending."</strong><br>
Individual cancellations are frequently local. The Virginia Digital Gateway — planned at roughly 2,100 acres and 22 million square feet, which would have made it the world's largest data centre campus — was abandoned by Blackstone-owned QTS after years of lawsuits, ultimately over a procedural notice issue. That is a land-use outcome, not a demand signal. Read the stated cause before drawing a conclusion about aggregate demand.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout callout-red">
<div class="callout-label">✗ Mistake 3</div>
<p class="callout-text"><strong>"Cancellation statistics show most capacity is being scrapped."</strong><br>
This is genuinely contested. Various 2026 tallies of blocked, delayed and cancelled US projects have circulated, and researchers at SemiAnalysis have publicly pushed back on the widely repeated claim that roughly half of 2026 US data centre capacity has been cancelled, arguing the figure conflates delayed, relocated and speculative announcements with genuine cancellations. Announced pipelines have always exceeded built capacity, so measuring cancellations against announcements overstates the shortfall. Treat any single headline number as one estimate, not a settled fact.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout callout-red">
<div class="callout-label">✗ Mistake 4</div>
<p class="callout-text"><strong>"Power companies are a safe way to play AI."</strong><br>
Utilities face multi-year interconnection queues, regulatory approval on rates, and public resistance to load growth that raises consumer bills. The demand is real and the revenue arrives slowly and subject to conditions a utility does not control. Rate-regulated utilities also cannot simply price to demand, which caps the upside that makes the trade attractive in the first place.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout callout-red">
<div class="callout-label">✗ Mistake 5</div>
<p class="callout-text"><strong>"Capex intensity is a sign of conviction, so it is bullish."</strong><br>
It is a sign of commitment, which cuts both ways. Capex converts into depreciation whether or not the asset earns a return, and at 46–54% of sales for three of the four largest spenders, that depreciation will weigh on reported margins for years. Investors have already begun scrutinising it — hyperscaler capex disclosures faced visible market pressure during the July 2026 reporting season.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout callout-red">
<div class="callout-label">✗ Mistake 6</div>
<p class="callout-text"><strong>"The chain moves together, so any exposure is equivalent."</strong><br>
The bottleneck shifts, and whoever holds it captures the margin. When memory is scarce, memory producers take the profit — visible in the fact that component price inflation accounts for roughly $25 billion of Microsoft's 2026 capex. That same inflation is a cost to the buyer. Positions on opposite sides of a bottleneck respond to the same news in opposite directions.</p>
</div>

## Example: When the Chain Runs in Reverse

The Virginia Digital Gateway in Prince William County, Virginia, was planned as approximately 2,100 acres and 22 million square feet of gigawatt-scale data centre capacity — the largest such campus proposed anywhere. Blackstone-owned QTS abandoned it after years of litigation.

<div class="callout-label">Key Insight</div>
<p class="callout-text">The important detail is <em>why</em> it died. Not financing failure, not weak AI demand, not a power shortfall — years of lawsuits ending in a procedural technicality over a newspaper notice. A project representing gigawatt-scale demand was removed from the pipeline by local land-use process. This is what makes the capex chain fragile in a way that has nothing to do with the technology: the revenue is contingent on permits, litigation and county votes, none of which appear in a supplier's order book or in an investor's model of AI adoption.</p>

## Frequently Asked Questions
- **What is capex and why does it matter for stock prices?**
  Capital expenditure is money spent on long-lived physical assets, recorded on the balance sheet and depreciated over years rather than expensed at once. It matters twice: for the spender, it is a commitment that becomes depreciation whether or not the asset earns a return; for suppliers, it is the source of future revenue. Because share prices discount expected future profits, a change in a large buyer's capex guidance moves supplier share prices immediately.

- **How much are the hyperscalers actually spending in 2026?**
  Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta plan roughly $725 billion combined, up about 77% from a record $410 billion in 2025. Individually: Amazon approximately $200 billion, Microsoft approximately $190 billion, Alphabet $180–190 billion, and Meta $125–145 billion after a mid-year increase. As a share of sales this represents roughly 54% at Meta, 47% at Microsoft, 46% at Alphabet and 25% at Amazon.

- **Which companies benefit most from AI capital spending?**
  The spending divides across three links: compute — accelerators, memory, storage, packaging and foundry capacity; power — generation, transmission, transformers, switchgear and cooling; and networking — optics, switches and interconnect. Compute converts orders to revenue fastest, typically within quarters. Power is slowest, constrained by multi-year grid interconnection queues that capital cannot accelerate.

- **What happens to supplier stocks when a data centre project is cancelled?**
  The expected future revenue is removed from the forecast, and because share prices discount expected profits rather than reported ones, the repricing can happen immediately even though no order was ever booked. The market frequently cannot distinguish a local cause from a demand signal in the moment, so an individual project failure can move the whole chain before the reason is understood.

- **Is the AI capex boom sustainable?**
  There is real disagreement and no settled answer. Supporting continuation: cloud revenue growth remains strong and the largest buyers have raised rather than cut guidance. Against it: capex at 46–54% of sales converts into depreciation regardless of return, investors began scrutinising hyperscaler capex disclosures during the July 2026 reporting season, and physical constraints — grid interconnection, permitting, local opposition — are binding in ways capital cannot solve.

- **Why did the Virginia Digital Gateway project fail?**
  Blackstone-owned QTS abandoned the roughly 2,100-acre, 22-million-square-foot campus in Prince William County, Virginia, after years of lawsuits, with the project ultimately failing over a procedural newspaper-notice technicality. The cause was land-use litigation rather than financing difficulty, weak demand or power availability. That distinction matters, because it means the cancellation carries no information about aggregate AI demand.

- **How do I avoid over-concentrating in the AI capex chain?**
  Check whether your holdings share a single dependency rather than counting them as separate positions. A chip designer, a memory producer, a utility with data centre load and a networking supplier are four tickers exposed to one assumption about hyperscaler spending. If that assumption changes, they reprice together. Examine each company's revenue exposure to the theme directly rather than relying on sector labels.
